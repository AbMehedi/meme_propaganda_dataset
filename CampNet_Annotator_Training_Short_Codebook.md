# CampNet: Short Annotator Training Codebook

**For:** first practice round with 17 image posts  
**Based on:** CampNet Annotation Guideline v1.0  
**Purpose:** help people learn the task before main annotation.

## 1. Your task

Look for a defined persuasion technique in each post. Persuasion means trying to influence what people think, feel, or do.

Read the original caption and the words inside the image. Then examine the picture. Choose a technique only when you can point to the words or picture details that support it.

**Main rule: show the evidence. Do not guess.**

- A post can have more than one technique, or none.
- Political content or criticism alone is not enough.
- A sad picture, flag, famous face, or insult alone is not enough. Check how it is used.
- A true statement can still use a persuasion technique. You are not checking whether the post is true or false.
- Do not label based on your political views or what you think the author intended.
- Check whether the post supports a message, quotes it neutrally, or rejects it. Quoting a technique does not automatically mean using it.
- Humor and satire can use techniques, but a funny picture alone is not enough.

The examples below are made for teaching. They are not labels for your selected images.

## 2. The seven main techniques

### T01 — Loaded Language

**Meaning:** words with strong positive or negative feelings are used to shape the reader's opinion.

**Example:** “এই ঘৃণ্য সিদ্ধান্ত মেনে নেওয়া যায় না।”  
“This disgusting decision cannot be accepted.”

**Evidence:** “ঘৃণ্য” adds strong negative judgment.

**Check:** which exact word adds emotional judgment beyond a plain description?

**Do not label:** “সিদ্ধান্তটি গতকাল প্রকাশিত হয়েছে।” (“The decision was published yesterday.”) This is plain reporting. A red background alone is also not Loaded Language; this technique needs words.

### T02 — Name Calling / Labeling

**Meaning:** a judgmental name or label is given to a person or group to shape how people see them.

**Example:** “ওরা জাতির শত্রু।”  
“They are enemies of the nation.”

**Evidence:** “জাতির শত্রু” is the label given to “ওরা”.

**Check:** what label is used, and who receives it? If the post does not identify “they”, record the target as unknown.

**Do not label:** “তিনি দলের সাধারণ সম্পাদক।” (“He is the party's general secretary.”) This is an ordinary role. An animal in a picture is not enough unless the post clearly connects it to a target as a degrading label.

### T03 — Smears

**Meaning:** a post damages a person's or group's reputation through an accusation, suggestion, or degrading connection.

**Example:** a fictional official named Rahim is shown with: “রহিম জনগণের টাকা নিজের পকেটে নিয়েছে।”  
“Rahim put public money into his own pocket.”

**Evidence:** the accusation of taking public money attacks Rahim's reputation. You do not decide whether the accusation is true.

**Check:** who is attacked, and what accusation or degrading picture connection attacks their reputation? A clear edited picture showing the target stealing from citizens may also qualify.

**Do not label:** “প্রকল্পের খরচের হিসাব প্রকাশ করুন।” (“Publish the project spending records.”) This asks for accountability without making an accusation. Do not add Smears to every insulting label; look for an additional reputation attack.

### T04 — Appeal to Fear / Prejudice

**Meaning:** danger or an existing negative belief about a group is used to push people to support or reject something.

**Example:** “ওদের সমর্থন করলে আপনার পরিবার কখনো নিরাপদ থাকবে না।”  
“If you support them, your family will never be safe.”

**Evidence:** the threat to family safety is linked to supporting “them”.

**Check:** what threat or prejudice is used? What choice does it push the audience toward?

**Do not label:** “নদীর পানি বাড়ছে; নিরাপদ স্থানে যান।” (“River water is rising; move to a safe place.”) A reasonable safety warning is not automatically this technique. A frightening picture also needs a link to the position or action being promoted.

### T05 — Exaggeration / Minimisation

**Meaning:** the message makes something seem much bigger, more certain, or less serious through its wording or presentation.

**Exaggeration example:** “একটি ভুলেই পুরো দেশ ধ্বংস হয়ে যাবে।”  
“One mistake will destroy the whole country.”

**Evidence:** the sweeping consequence, “পুরো দেশ ধ্বংস”.

**Minimisation example:** the post shows serious destruction but dismisses it with “কিছুই হয়নি, সামান্য ব্যাপার।” (“Nothing happened; it is a small matter.”)

**Check:** what is inflated or played down? Can you explain this from the post itself?

**Do not label:** “সভায় প্রায় এক হাজার মানুষ ছিল।” (“About one thousand people attended.”) An unchecked number is not enough. Words like “all” or “never”, a large portrait, or a zoomed photograph are clues, not automatic proof.

### T06 — Slogans

**Meaning:** a short, memorable rallying phrase is used to gather support or encourage action.

**Example:** “এক দাবি, এক লক্ষ্য—অন্যায় রুখে দাঁড়াও!”  
“One demand, one goal—stand against injustice!”

**Evidence:** the phrase is used as a call to unite and act.

**Check:** is the post using the phrase to rally people? Does the post support it, or only report or reject it?

**Do not label:** “আজ বিকেলে বৃষ্টি হয়েছে।” (“It rained this afternoon.”) A short sentence is not automatically a slogan. A news card that neutrally quotes a slogan may not be using it as its own appeal.

### T07 — Appeal to Strong Emotions

**Meaning in CampNet:** a picture is used to create strong pity, grief, admiration, anger, or another feeling to support a position.

**Example:** a picture of a distressed child is paired with “এই শিশুদের জন্য আমাদের আন্দোলনকে সমর্থন করুন।”  
“Support our movement for these children.”

**Evidence:** the child's distress is used to encourage support for the movement.

**Check:** what picture detail creates the feeling, and how does it support the message?

**Do not label:** a sad photograph in a plain news report without a supported persuasive link. Emotional words alone do not qualify for T07 under this codebook; consider T01. For a threat-based appeal, consider T04. Do not add T07 just to count the same fear appeal twice.

## 3. Common differences

| If the main evidence is… | Consider… |
| --- | --- |
| Emotionally judgmental words | T01 — Loaded Language |
| A judgmental name given to someone | T02 — Name Calling / Labeling |
| An accusation or degrading connection damaging reputation | T03 — Smears |
| A threat or prejudice linked to a choice | T04 — Appeal to Fear / Prejudice |
| Inflated or dismissed scale, importance, or consequences | T05 — Exaggeration / Minimisation |
| A memorable phrase used to rally people | T06 — Slogans |
| A picture's emotional appeal linked to a position | T07 — Appeal to Strong Emotions |

More than one label is allowed, but explain the evidence for each. Do not add labels just because their meanings are related.

## 4. What to record

For each technique, record:

1. **Technique:** ID and name.
2. **Words:** copy the exact supporting words. Say whether they come from the caption or inside the image. If none, say so.
3. **Picture evidence:** describe and mark the smallest useful area. If the link uses two areas, mark both.
4. **Reason:** one simple sentence connecting the evidence to the definition.
5. **Picture's role:** choose from the table below.
6. **Confidence:** 1 = uncertain; 2 = borderline but supported; 3 = clear evidence.

**Words inside an image are still text evidence.** A face, object, action, or symbol can be picture evidence. Large letters or text placement should be recorded separately as typography/layout when they matter.

| Picture's role | Simple test |
| --- | --- |
| `REQUIRED` | Without the relevant picture detail, the text cannot support this same technique instance. |
| `REINFORCING` | The text already supports it, and the picture strengthens it. Explain how. |
| `NOT_USED` | The technique is supported by text; the picture gives no relevant support. |
| `UNRESOLVED` | You cannot decide the picture's role confidently. |

If the picture's role is unclear but the technique is clear, keep the technique and mark only the picture's role unresolved.

## 5. None, unsure, and other techniques

- **No main technique:** after reviewing the whole post, no T01–T07 is supported. Record an empty label list. This is not an eighth technique.
- **Unsure:** unreadable words or missing context prevent a decision. Mark `UNRESOLVED` and explain. Do not use “none” to mean “I do not know”.
- **Outside the task:** a corrupt, out-of-scope, or unsupported multi-image post should be marked `EXCLUDE` with a reason.
- **Other possible technique:** record it separately. Do not force it into a main label.

Watchlist notes are optional candidates, not main labels:

| Candidate | Look for | Not enough alone |
| --- | --- | --- |
| W01 — Flag-Waving / Group-Identity Appeal | National or group belonging used as a reason to support something | A flag |
| W02 — Transfer | A person's or symbol's good/bad association clearly linked to a target | A famous face |
| W03 — Glittering Generalities / Virtue | Attractive values attached to a target to invite support without meaningful explanation | A word such as “peace” |

## 6. Practice with your 17 posts

1. Read this codebook together. Discuss the teaching examples before labeling the selected posts.
2. For each post, first read the checked image text and original caption **without seeing the image**. Save your text-only labels and evidence.
3. Then view the full post. Make a new decision. You can add or remove labels; keep your earlier text-only record unchanged.
4. Each annotator works alone before discussion. Do not show AI suggestions or another person's labels during this first practice round.
5. Compare the saved decisions. Discuss every disagreement using the definitions and evidence. A designated reviewer records the agreed decision or marks it unresolved.
6. Write down unclear rules and missing examples. Revise the codebook before the next annotation round.

These 17 posts are a learning and calibration round. They may not contain every technique. Do not force a label to fill a category. Keep training examples out of your final held-out evaluation set.

**Before submitting:** Can I point to the evidence? Does it match the definition? Did I check quotation or rejection? Did I record uncertainty instead of guessing?
